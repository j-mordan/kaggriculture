This was my attempt at the Kaggriculture Kaggle Competition hosted by Google, I think. This was my first competition of this type and I made a few agents, but unfortunately was unable to reach my goal of top 20%, fluctuating up around 50% at the end (5,500th out of 10,246). Although I did not reach my goal, I gained valuable experience and learned the importance of planning out system design so that it could be easily built upon to tackle new problems, and I will take this with me for future competitions or projects.
A summary of my strategy was to assign each farmer to a column and focus entirely on that since each agent is given observation data each turn and cannot save data between turns. This makes it hard to plan what a worker does, for example if one turn agent decides on an optimal route for a worker, it cannot save its intentions, and on subsequent turns new variables such as a changed position can change the plan, causing inefficient routing. I had hoped that using a column based approach would minimize the amount of loss due to farmers not being able to tend to every crop planted.
The product_values function takes into account many factors, including current inventory, amount of crop currently in play by both players, and amount intended to purchase, in order to predict the sell price of each crop to find out which crop makes the most profit per day, so it should be planted. Additionally, dynamic programming is used to solve the knapsack problem occurring when the agent wants to buy the most profitable but also more expensive crops, causing it to run out of money before utilizing all of the tiles, leaving profit on the table, as one cow may be more profitable than one wheat individually, but 20 wheat costs less than the cow but fills more tiles and makes more money overall.
One of the things I did that I think is unique is my strategy for melons. Melons are unique because they have very little demand and basically are only valuable at the start of the game until they are quickly over farmed usually by turn 11 and the price drops to below 100. When day 11 ends, the melons planted on day 1 are put into the shed from worker inventories and are sold unit by unit alternating between players. I thought it would be good to on turn 11 invest in another farmer who goes around to the tiles near the shed and harvests the melons, and then drops them in the shed so that my melons would be sold first, giving me the peak melon price and also tanking the price for my opponent for when they sell. This strategy was successful to some degree and usually gave me an advantage heading into mid/late game.
